## Phase 5 (projection and distortion)

- **5-1, 2026-10-18, user.** M0 (browser memory at 2.56M) accepted. Reductions R1 to R6 approved, each a separate commit; R7 (allocation-free `basis()` in the climate downscaling) approved and done; **R8 deferred to Phase 7**: skip orogen's own stage 1 to 3 fields at N where the detail pass replaces them. It touches `packages/gen-orogen/src/port/elevation.ts`, so it needs its own approval and parity tests; it was not investigated. Findings: WebKit keeps dead allocations in proportion to allocation volume; the detail pass allocated 7.43 GB and now 2.65 GB (−64%); every output stayed bit-identical (nine worlds); WebKit engine worker 6,718 MB → 5,219 MB against stock 4,383 MB (Mac, M5 Pro). Full record: `docs/checkpoints/PHASE5_M0_MEMORY.md`.
- **5-2, 2026-10-18, user.** d3 determinism: a build-time alias gives the export and CPU-sampler path a second copy of d3-geo and d3-geo-projection whose `math.js` calls `dmath`; d3's files are never modified; interactive display keeps stock d3 with native math. Tolerances against stock d3 in fdlibm mode: 8 ULP forward, 32 ULP inverse, 128 ULP for Hammer's inverse (measured 103). Alias files are in `packages/projection/alias/` (not `build/`, which `.gitignore` excludes).
- **5-3, 2026-10-18, user.** Heightmap scales: orogen's two mappings (−5 to +6 km, and land-only 0 to 6 km) plus a custom range in metres; the mapping and range are written into the PNG as text. Mapping to grey happens after sampling in metres (cloud recommendation, accepted).
- **5-4, 2026-10-19, cloud (included in the M3 prompt).** Pixels outside a projection's outline: plain 16-bit greyscale (colour type 0) by default with outside pixels = 0, recorded in the PNG text; transparent-outside (grey plus alpha, type 4) is an option. Reason: most height-map consumers expect single-channel 16-bit. M2 had made type 4 the default; the golden hashes for the `fd.projection` probe keys were recorded in this phase, so re-recording them is expected and must be stated in the commit. Done in `ca50aae`.
- **5-5, 2026-10-18, user.** GPU shader projections: equirectangular, Mercator, Equal Earth, Mollweide, Natural Earth 1, Robinson, orthographic, stereographic, azimuthal equal-area, azimuthal equidistant. Every other projection uses the CPU path at reduced resolution.
- **5-6, 2026-10-18, agent.** Distortion at line and cusp poles uses extrapolation with an order estimated by Aitken's Δ² process instead of §3.7 rule 3's fixed "grows by more than 2× per halving" test, which misses slow divergences such as Mollweide's k ∝ δ^(−1/3) at its cusp (measured: 19.46 reported instead of ∞). Documented in `packages/projection/src/distortion.ts`. Done: §3.7 rule 3 carries a one-line correction (`2e77a5a`).
- **5-7, 2026-10-18, agent.** `rawScale` in the projection registry makes d3's stereographic (×2) and Robinson (×0.8487) raws true to their published definitions, so distortion values are absolute.
- **5-8, 2026-10-19, agent.** Exported vector geometry is resampled at 0.05 px (`EXPORT_PRECISION_PX`) instead of d3's √0.5 px: at the default, a Tissot circle split by Hammer's antimeridian lost 3.7% of its area (0.2% at 0.05 px). Interactive views keep d3's default. Reversible (one constant).
- **5-9, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** GPU shaders do not use the GPU's built-in transcendental functions. Measured in Chromium (ANGLE on Metal): about 4e-4 absolute error, which displaced content by 0.1 to 0.6 px at 16× zoom and 0.5 px at the orthographic limb at 1× (visible). The shader carries float32 Cephes algorithms (sin, cos, atan, atan2, asin, acos, exp, log: Cody-Waite reduction and minimax polynomials); only sqrt and arithmetic come from the GPU. Alternatives: split-float (double-float) arithmetic (much slower, unnecessary once libm is accurate); CPU refinement (defeats the GPU path). Reversible.
- **5-10, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** Where d3's formula cancels in float32, the shader uses a mathematically identical rearrangement: the azimuthal inverses return the raw unit vector (cos c, x sin c / z, y sin c / z) instead of atan2/asin; the azimuthal equidistant and equal-area forwards take sin c from the vector components instead of from cos c (antipode); Mollweide's inverse goes through u = π − 2|θ| with a series for u − sin u (poles). The round trip follows d3's rotation semantics: with a φ or γ rotation d3 recomputes λ from the vector (so at a raw pole λ is lost and pixels just beyond a pole line are outside), otherwise λ is kept. Visibility walk: stepping back to the previous triangle means the point is on the shared edge within float32 noise, and the walk stops (seen once: a ping-pong in Firefox/WebKit orthographic).
- **5-11, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** Measured GPU against CPU (20k world, 10 projections × 7 views each including both polar aspects and 16× zooms on the antimeridian and next to a pole; Chromium, Firefox, WebKit on macOS arm64): screen displacement of the shader's point against d3's ≤ 0.0066 px at 1× and ≤ 0.0012 px at 16× (tolerances 0.02 and 0.05 px); in/out differences only within 0.02 px of the 0.5 px round-trip threshold, 1e-5 rad of a clip circle, Mercator limit or raw pole (0 elsewhere); triangle differs from the CPU sampler at ≤ 0.013% of pixels and nearest cell at ≤ 0.0063%, every one explained by float32 noise at an edge (the shader's triangle contains the shader's point to 1e-5 in normalised weight; its cell is nearest to it to 1e-6 rad); value error ≤ 1.6e-4 of the layer's range where the triangle agrees; walks ≤ 7 steps (bound 64). Float32 is therefore invisible at every zoom the app allows (1× to 16×). Cell positions are uploaded as float32 (an edge moves by at most 3e-8 rad, 0.2 m on Earth).
- **5-12, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** Lookup data: one cube-map texel per triangle (3R × 2R, R = ⌈√(triangles/6)⌉; above cell density as §3.7 asks), built in the engine worker (3.4 s at 2.56M cells, measured in Node), walks ≤ 7 steps. Barycentrics relative to the triangle's first vertex (no cancellation at the triangle's scale). Flat data textures are R32UI/R32F, row width = the GPU's MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE; a mesh that does not fit (for example 2.56M cells on a GPU limited to 2048) falls back to the CPU path.
- **5-13, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** Flat-map colours: barycentric layers interpolate the value and then apply the palette in the shader (orogen's `elevationToColor` and the globe's 4-stop ramp, ported), so a coastline is a sharp contour inside a triangle rather than a colour blend; categorical layers (Köppen, plates) take the nearest cell's colour. Output passes three.js's linear → sRGB transfer, as the globe's material does, so both views show the same colours. Vector-magnitude layers (wind, currents) interpolate the per-cell magnitude.
- **5-14, 2026-10-19, agent (M4).** Projections without a shader draw on the CPU path at half the linear resolution, sampled in the engine worker in bands of 16 rows (`projection.locate`), colourised on the main thread with the shader's rules; a newer render supersedes an older one. The flat map zooms from 1× to 16×.
- **5-15, 2026-10-19, agent (M5).** Flat-map UI: the controls sit above the map (globe/flat switch next to the layer select). Wheel zoom keeps the point under the cursor fixed; drag pans only when zoomed; changing projection resets zoom and pan, not rotation. Interactive overlays (graticule, coastlines, Tissot, outline) are SVG from stock d3 at the map's device-pixel size; the distortion heatmap is computed in the worker at a quarter of the linear resolution, one request at a time (the latest state is drawn when it finishes). Exports are always the whole outline at zoom 1 with the current projection and rotation, at a chosen width (height from the outline's aspect), through the exact path in the worker; zoomed-window export is not offered. Reversible.

## Product

- **P1, 2026-10-19, cloud.** No seed reproduces Earth's actual coastlines: the seed becomes a state of at most about 2.1 billion values (`vendor/js/rng.js`: modulo 2,147,483,646), and a coarse match to Earth's layout has odds of roughly 1 in 10¹⁵ per world (a rough illustration, not a measurement). "Earth conditions give back Earth" means statistical similarity (T1), pinned real data (T2) and deep-time statistics (T3), per `docs/design/15-earth-test-suite.md`. Exact geography comes from importing real data (Phase 11). **To do:** state this in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` and note 15.
- **P2, 2026-10-19, cloud.** Earth-like defaults: `OROGEN_DEFAULTS` are **not** changed (that would change world hashes and baselines). Instead a named "Earth-like" preset is produced by a sweep of the 14 sliders against Earth statistics (`tools/tuning/earthlike/`, report `docs/earth-tests/DEFAULTS_SWEEP.md`), added to the settings panel as a preset. Evidence so far supports only: land coverage 0.30 (measured land fraction 31.3% against Earth's 31.2%), temperature offset 0, precipitation offset 0, jitter 0.75. Continents 6 to 7, size variety 0.5 to 0.7 and plates about 40 are unmeasured guesses. Repeat the sweep after Phase 6b and redo it for all parameters in Phase 8. Spec for the tool: `docs/RESUME.md` queue item Q3.
- **P3, 2026-10-19, user (report), cloud (plan).** Defect: generated continents can look unrealistically rectangular even with organic settings. Not diagnosed. Plan: reproduce over several seeds, measure the share of coastline in long near-straight segments and each landmass's fit to an axis-aligned box, find the origin (coarse plate and land-mask stage, the 20k reference mesh, the detail pass, or rendering). Queue item Q2. Phase 6b may also change shapes, but do not wait for it.
- **P4, 2026-10-19, cloud.** Desktop software: Electron wrapping the same bundle (Q4), as an optional packaging step after Checkpoint 5c (installers on GitHub Releases); Phase 14 still decides the performance case. Reason: Electron is Chromium, which avoids WebKit's memory behaviour (Chromium 2.2 GB against WebKit about 6 GB at 2.56M cells). Queue item Q4.
